# Somnath

This was another of those trips that came together around an important date. My birthday was approaching, and as with New Year and other important calendar days, I liked the idea of spending it away from home. This time the destination was Somnath, because of the Jyotirlinga.

We flew from Delhi to Ahmedabad on 14 March 2014. The flight arrived around 6 in the evening, and there was very little time before our overnight sleeper bus to Somnath. The bus had been booked through RedBus; at that point we knew little more than the boarding point. We went straight there, had some food before boarding, and found ourselves waiting at what felt like a rather deserted spot, with just one shop and not many passengers around.

The sleeper bus made the overnight journey surprisingly easy. Most of the time was spent sleeping, and we reached Somnath early the next morning, around 7. It was a cold morning. We had not booked a hotel, so I asked the auto driver to take us around to a few places before we finally settled into Hotel Ambar.

The main reason for coming to Somnath was, of course, the Somnath Jyotirlinga. We visited the temple in the evening and also visited a few local temples and places nearby. At one of the nearby temples we were able to do Rudrabhishekam, which was not allowed at Somnath itself.

Somnath itself felt beautifully calm. It was a full moon night, and there was something very peaceful about being there under that moon after the long journey from Delhi.

We also visited the spot associated with the belief that Lord Krishna left the earthly world after being struck by an arrow. These smaller visits around Somnath became part of the journey along with the main temple visit.

Unfortunately, the food did not suit Pragati and she fell sick. By the time we were leaving Somnath, I was roaming around looking for a pharmacy to get medicines for her. It was Holi that day and most shops were closed. Luckily, I managed to find one that was open.

On the night of 16 March we boarded the sleeper bus back to Ahmedabad. Again, it was an overnight journey and we reached Ahmedabad the next morning. Since Pragati was still not feeling well, the pace was slow. I went out on my own to visit Adalaj Vav, something I had found by Googling at the last moment. It had not been part of the original plan, but it gave me some time to walk around and take photographs.

We also visited Sabarmati Ashram, where I spent time going through the different places in the museum. Ahmedabad had now become more than just the place through which we were travelling because of the flights. It had become part of the birthday trip itself.

My birthday was actually celebrated in Ahmedabad. There wasn't much of a celebration — just a simple dinner at a restaurant in the Holiday Inn where we were staying. Pragati wasn't feeling like eating, so I went down alone for dinner. While there, I noticed Parthiv Patel, the Indian wicketkeeper-batsman, also having dinner. I didn't want to disturb him while he was eating, so I waited until he had finished and then politely asked him for a selfie. He obliged.

The next day, 18 March, we flew back to Delhi.

It was a simple birthday trip, built around a place I had wanted to visit for its religious significance, with two overnight sleeper-bus journeys, a full-moon evening at Somnath, an unplanned photographic stop at Adalaj Vav, and an unexpected meeting over dinner in Ahmedabad. In the end, Ahmedabad became as much a part of the memory as Somnath itself.
